# CBL Foundational Ideas and Design Principles

## Summary

The CBL framework "builds on a set of foundational ideas" — persistent orientations that inform the entire approach, not steps in a process (CBL Guide 2016, p. 8). The Guide presents twelve such **foundational ideas** in its Foundations section (pp. 8–10). Familiarity with these concepts "provides deeper insight into the framework, opportunities for discussion, and support for implementation" (p. 8).

## Guide-Backed Origin

The Guide reports that CBL emerged from the ACOT² project, initiated in 2008 to identify essential design principles for a 21st-century learning environment. It says those principles informed subsequent work with exemplary educators to develop and test CBL (CBL Guide 2016, p. 6). The Guide's resource appendix describes six ACOT² design principles as having "set the foundation for Challenge Based Learning" (p. 52). It does not map the twelve foundational ideas one-to-one onto those six principles; the ideas are the framework's own articulation of its foundations.

## The Twelve Foundational Ideas

All names and definitions below follow the guide's Foundations section (CBL Guide 2016, pp. 8–10).

### 1. Everyone is a Learner: Teacher/Learner and Learner/Teacher
Ubiquitous access to information creates the opportunity to break down the traditional hierarchical structure of learning environments. All stakeholders become both teachers and learners, actively sharing the responsibility (and workload) for the learning experience — without diminishing the role of teachers, who "still have the primary responsibility for a successful learning experience" (p. 8).

### 2. Moving Beyond the Four Walls of the Classroom
Involving all community members expands resources, creates opportunities for authentic learning, and moves the responsibility of education to the larger community (p. 9).

### 3. Learner Inspired, Learner Directed
Meaningful connections are made between content and the lives of learners. The more passionate learners are about the content, the deeper the learning; the more control all participants have over the process, the higher the level of ownership (p. 9). *Practice observation: this ownership dimension is a useful differentiator from problem-based learning variants where problems are provided externally — a comparison the guide itself does not make in these terms (its PBL comparison, pp. 45–46, centres on the origin of the project and the role of the teacher).*

### 4. Challenges
"Situations or activities that create a sense of urgency and spur action" (p. 9). A challenge is not a question to answer but a condition to change.

### 5. Content and 21st Century Skills
Authentic learning experiences foster in-depth content knowledge and help students organically develop 21st-century skills. "These skills do not become 'one more thing' to be addressed by the teacher but emerge from the Challenge experience" (p. 9). Both are valued — CBL is not skills-at-the-expense-of-content.

### 6. Boundaries of Adventure
Boundaries are provided to guide the way and provide freedom for learners to take ownership of the process. At the beginning the limits will be narrow, "but the goal is always to move towards more freedom and responsibility for the Learner" (p. 9).

### 7. Space and Freedom to Fail
A safe space for all learners to think creatively, try new ideas, experiment, fail, receive feedback, and try again. "All of the phases of the framework include opportunities for iteration" (p. 9).

### 8. Slowing for Critical and Creative Thinking
"To ensure full participation and to provide opportunities for deep thinking, the learning process is intentionally slowed down at times" (p. 9). This is counter to efficiency-oriented instruction.

### 9. Authentic and Powerful Use of Technology
Technology is used to research, communicate, organize, create, and present information. "The use of technology allows Learners to own and transform the learning experience" (p. 10). Technology is not the point — it is a tool for the work.

### 10. Focus on Process and Product
"The journey to the solution is valued as much as the solution" — throughout the CBL experience there are opportunities to evaluate and assess both process and products (p. 10). This principle legitimizes productive struggle and partial solutions.

### 11. Documentation
During each step of the Challenge process, learners document and publish using text, video, audio, and pictures. These artifacts are useful for ongoing reflection, informative assessment, evidence of learning, portfolios, and telling the story of their Challenge (p. 10).

### 12. Reflection
Throughout the process, learners continuously reflect on the content and the process. "Much of the deepest learning takes place by considering the process, thinking about one's learning, and analyzing ongoing relationships between the content and concepts" (p. 10).

## Structural Tensions Worth Noting

*Practice observation (corpus-author analysis; not sourced from the CBL documentation):* several foundational ideas are in creative tension:
- *Slowing for critical and creative thinking* vs. *Challenges spur action* — CBL holds both, relying on the practitioner to modulate pace
- *Learner directed* vs. *Boundaries of Adventure* — autonomy within structure
- *Space to fail* vs. *authentic audience* — public stakes and permission to fail simultaneously

## Notes on Meta-Skills

> **Corpus annotation (2026-07-09):** Foundational ideas #12 (Reflection) and #8 (Slowing for Critical and Creative Thinking) are the closest the CBL Guide 2016 comes to a meta-skills orientation. However, they are framed as *process dispositions*, not as explicitly taught competencies. The phrase "learn how to learn" does **not** appear in the guide; its most direct reference to a meta-learning orientation is the Welcome text's goal of "developing a framework for life-long learning" (CBL Guide 2016, p. 4), which is not operationalized into discrete skills students practice. This is a meaningful gap if the intent of a CBL tool is to assess or develop meta-level learning competency. It would require extension beyond the official framework.
