# Fictional Illustrations of Three CBE Maturity Profiles

> **Fictional illustrations.** Every profile in this document is fictional. Institution names, stakeholder arrangements and apparent student feedback are parts of the supplied illustrations, not evidence that the named institution or PIONEER delivered the module or observed the stated outcomes.

## Summary

The three profiles show a content-supported progression across the existing PIONEER continuum in `(corpus:methodology-007 § The three maturity levels and their synonyms)`: `minimal` is Level 1, `fully` is Level 2, and `good_practice` / `VISION not real` is Level 3. The filenames do not override that fixed mapping. These profiles illustrate differences in challenge openness, stakeholder roles, learner agency, disciplinary integration, iteration, reflection and intended outcomes. They add no thresholds, empirical effectiveness claims or workbook assessment.

## Level 3 — Fictional vision profile

**Profile:** European Urban Living Labs / VISION not real

This is a fictional illustration, not a record of delivery or observed institutional outcomes.

### Level 3 — Module Identity

#### Level 3 — Module Title

European Urban Living Labs: Co-Creating Climate-Resilient Neighbourhoods

#### Level 3 — Short Description

Working in international and interdisciplinary teams across several PIONEER partner universities, students address comparable urban challenges in different cities. Together with municipalities, community organisations, and citizens, they combine academic and non-academic knowledge to co-create, test, and compare locally adapted solutions for more resilient and liveable neighbourhoods. The module combines local challenge-solving with recurring international exchange phases in which student teams compare findings, reflect on contextual differences, and transfer ideas across cities.

#### Level 3 — University / Institution

PIONEER Alliance (fictional cross-alliance module)

#### Level 3 — Program Level

BA / MA

#### Level 3 — Program / Discipline

Interdisciplinary and transdisciplinary (Urban Studies, Environmental Sciences, Engineering, Social Sciences, Business, ICT, together with municipal and community stakeholders)

#### Level 3 — ECTS Credits

10

#### Level 3 — Duration

12 weeks

#### Level 3 — Cohort Size

30 (MA) –60 (BA) students

### Level 3 — Delivery Architecture

#### Level 3 — Delivery Form

Blended

#### Level 3 — Delivery Format

Semester Module / Challenge-Based Project

#### Level 3 — Institutional Constraints

Assessment regulations, academic calendar, stakeholder availability, interdisciplinary scheduling, language requirements.

#### Level 3 — Design Constraints

Mixed disciplinary student teams

Availability of municipal stakeholders

Limited implementation budget

Alignment with semester timelines

Coordination across participating programmes

### Level 3 — Challenge & Stakeholders

#### Level 3 — Challenge Topic

Climate-resilient and inclusive neighbourhoods in European cities (SDG 11)

#### Level 3 — Challenge Formulation

How can European cities adapt public spaces and neighbourhoods to increasing climate risks while ensuring accessibility, social inclusion, and quality of life for diverse groups of citizens?

#### Level 3 — Challenge Authenticity / Origin

Authentic challenges originating from several municipalities within the PIONEER Alliance. Each participating city contributes a local urban challenge related to climate adaptation and liveability. Student teams collaborate internationally to identify common patterns, compare approaches, and develop context-sensitive solutions that can be adapted to different local settings.

#### Level 3 — Challenge Owner / Stakeholders

Challenge Owners: Municipal departments for urban development from participating PIONEER cities.

Stakeholders: Municipal planners, environmental agencies, neighbourhood associations, citizens, youth representatives, senior citizens’ councils, local businesses, NGOs, and urban development experts.

Stakeholders are involved not only as challenge providers and feedback partners but as co-creators throughout the challenge process. Together with students and teachers, they contribute to challenge framing, the definition of success criteria, the evaluation of proposed interventions, and the identification of pathways for implementation.

#### Level 3 — Stakeholder Involvement Moments

Joint challenge definition workshop with municipal partners.

Local stakeholder mapping and field observations.

Interviews and dialogue sessions with citizens and community representatives.

International challenge exchange workshops where teams present local findings, compare stakeholder perspectives, identify common patterns, and adapt promising ideas from partner cities.

Co-design workshops involving municipal representatives, citizens, and students.

Prototype testing and validation with local stakeholders.

Cross-city reflection workshop on transferability of solutions.

Final presentation and discussion with challenge owners, stakeholders, and community representatives.

### Level 3 — Constructive Alignment

#### Level 3 — Intended Learning Outcomes

Learners are able to develop, test, and evaluate interventions for climate-resilient and inclusive neighbourhoods (what) by collaboratively analysing authentic urban challenges, engaging stakeholders in co-creative processes, and integrating academic and non-academic perspectives in international teams (how) in order to create societally relevant solutions and assess their transferability across different urban contexts. (why)

#### Level 3 — Key Learning Activities

Participating in a European kick-off event introducing the common challenge and the participating cities.

Exploring local urban challenges through stakeholder mapping, field observations, interviews, and data collection.

Collaboratively analysing the challenge by integrating academic knowledge with stakeholder perspectives and local expertise.

Exchanging findings with international student teams and comparing similarities, differences, and contextual factors across cities.

Co-designing intervention concepts together with local stakeholders through workshops and feedback sessions.

Developing and testing prototypes, scenarios, or implementation concepts in collaboration with stakeholders.

Evaluating the feasibility, societal relevance, and potential impact of proposed interventions.

Assessing the transferability of solutions across different urban contexts through international peer review and comparison.

Presenting results to stakeholders and participating in a European showcase event.

Reflecting on interdisciplinary collaboration, stakeholder engagement, ethical considerations, and personal learning.

#### Level 3 — Assessment Criteria / Rubrics

Challenge analysis and stakeholder engagement – 25%

Ability to understand the challenge context, identify relevant stakeholders, integrate multiple perspectives, and engage stakeholders appropriately throughout the project.

Intervention design and testing – 30%

Quality, feasibility, creativity, and evidence base of the proposed intervention, including testing and refinement.

International collaboration and transferability analysis – 20%

Quality of collaboration across international teams and ability to analyse similarities, differences, and transferability of solutions across urban contexts.

Reflection and professional development – 25%

Critical reflection on learning processes, interdisciplinary collaboration, stakeholder engagement, ethical considerations, and personal development.

#### Level 3 — Assessment Evidence & Feedback

Group submission: Local challenge analysis report including stakeholder map, field research findings, and problem framing as well as jointly developed success criteria.

Group submission: Intervention concept, prototype, policy recommendation, or implementation proposal developed in response to the local challenge.

Group submission: Transferability analysis comparing findings, assumptions, and possible solutions across participating cities.

Individual submission: Reflection portfolio focusing on interdisciplinary collaboration, stakeholder engagement, ethical tensions, and professional learning.

Feedback is provided at several points: Teacher feedback on the challenge analysis, stakeholder feedback during co-design and testing, international peer feedback during cross-city exchange sessions, and joint evaluation by teachers and challenge owners during the European final showcase.

#### Level 3 — Reflection Design

Reflection is integrated throughout the module rather than added only at the end. Students reflect individually and in teams after the local research phase, after the international comparison sessions, after stakeholder feedback, and after the final presentation. The reflection focuses on student agency, interdisciplinary teamwork, stakeholder collaboration, ethical and societal trade-offs, and the transferability of solutions across different urban contexts.

### Level 3 — Phase-by-Phase Walkthrough

#### Level 3 — Phase 1 – Engage

European kick-off event introducing the common challenge, participating cities, and local challenge owners.

Formation of interdisciplinary teams and introduction to challenge-based learning, stakeholder engagement, and urban climate resilience.

Joint challenge definition workshops with local stakeholders.

Students, stakeholders, and teachers collaboratively refine the challenge focus, identify key questions, and define preliminary success criteria that will guide the project.

#### Level 3 — Phase 2 – Investigate

Local stakeholder mapping, field observations, interviews, and data collection.

Analysis of the local challenge from academic, professional, and community perspectives.

European mid-term exchange event where teams present findings, compare stakeholder perspectives, discuss contextual differences, and receive feedback from peers and challenge owners.

#### Level 3 — Phase 3 – Act / Prototype

Co-design workshops with stakeholders.

Development and testing of intervention concepts, prototypes, policy recommendations, or implementation scenarios.

Refinement of solutions based on local stakeholder feedback and insights gained from the European exchange.

#### Level 3 — Phase 4 – Reflect & Present

European final showcase bringing together students, teachers, challenge owners, and stakeholders from all participating cities.

Presentation of local solutions, discussion of common patterns and contextual differences, and assessment of transferability across cities.

Stakeholders, students, and teachers jointly evaluate the proposed interventions and discuss their feasibility, societal value, and implementation potential.

Joint reflection on societal impact, stakeholder collaboration, interdisciplinary learning, and future implementation pathways.

Documentation of recommendations for participating municipalities and future challenge cycles.

Where feasible, selected interventions may enter pilot implementation, follow-up projects, or municipal planning processes after completion of the module.

### Level 3 — Instructor Perspective

#### Level 3 — Preparation Required

Establish partnerships with municipalities and local stakeholders before the start of the module and jointly identify suitable urban challenges. Coordinate the common challenge framework, milestones, and exchange formats with participating PIONEER universities. Prepare students for interdisciplinary collaboration, stakeholder engagement, and challenge-based learning through introductory activities and shared resources.

#### Level 3 — Instructor Role During Module

Act as facilitator, coach, and connector rather than primarily as a content expert. Support students in framing challenges, engaging stakeholders, navigating uncertainty, and reflecting on their learning. Coordinate international exchange activities and help students connect local findings with insights from partner cities. Facilitate constructive dialogue between academic and non-academic actors throughout the challenge process.

#### Level 3 — Challenges & Tips

Aligning timelines, expectations, and communication across multiple institutions and stakeholder groups requires careful planning. Students may initially struggle with the openness of the challenge and the complexity of stakeholder perspectives. Regular milestones, structured reflection activities, and clearly defined stakeholder roles help maintain focus and momentum. International exchange sessions are most effective when directly linked to ongoing project decisions rather than treated as stand-alone events.

#### Level 3 — Variations / Adaptations

The format can be implemented at different levels of complexity. A lighter version may involve a single university working with local stakeholders while incorporating international case comparisons. More advanced versions may include joint international student teams, physical mobility components, shared stakeholder workshops across cities, or collaborative development of transferable solutions for multiple urban contexts. The challenge topic may also be adapted to other SDG-related issues such as mobility, social inclusion, energy transition, or circular economy.

### Level 3 — Outcomes & Transferability

#### Level 3 — Student Experience

Students experience the complexity of real-world societal challenges and learn to work across disciplinary, institutional, and cultural boundaries. Through direct collaboration with stakeholders and peers from other European universities, they develop confidence in dealing with ambiguity, balancing competing interests, and co-creating context-sensitive solutions. The combination of local action and international exchange helps students understand both the uniqueness of local contexts and the broader relevance of shared societal challenges.

#### Level 3 — Transferability

The module design can be transferred to a wide range of disciplines and societal challenges. While this example focuses on climate-resilient and inclusive neighbourhoods (SDG 11), the same structure can be applied to topics such as sustainable mobility, energy transition, social inclusion, public health, circular economy, or digital transformation. The combination of local stakeholder engagement and recurring international exchange moments offers a scalable model for Challenge-Based Education within European university alliances and beyond. Particularly transferable is the learning architecture itself: a common societal challenge is explored locally, enriched through international comparison, refined through stakeholder collaboration, and brought together in a shared European learning and impact process.
