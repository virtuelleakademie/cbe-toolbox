# Constructive Alignment in CBE: The Goals × Activities × Assessment Triad

## Summary

Constructive alignment (Biggs) holds that intended competencies, learning activities, and assessment must form a coherent triad; the Alignment Check audits the triad and flags the recurring patterns where one corner contradicts another.

## The triad

Constructive alignment treats a module as coherent only when three corners agree:

1. **Goals** — the intended competencies (sharpened by the Competence Check).
2. **Activities** — what learners actually do (the CBE challenge work).
3. **Assessment** — what is measured and rewarded.

Mis-alignment is the normal failure mode, not the exception: each corner is usually
authored at a different time, and drift between them is invisible until a learner
optimises for the assessment rather than the goal.

## Mis-alignment-pattern catalogue

The Alignment Check scans for these recurring patterns:

- **Process goal, product assessment.** The goal names a process competence
  (collaboration, iterative design, research synthesis) but the rubric weights the
  final artefact almost entirely. Learners rationally skip the process.
- **Verb–deliverable mismatch.** The goal/challenge verb ("design", "evaluate")
  does not match the deliverable the assessment actually scores ("a report").
- **Unassessed goal.** A stated competence has no corresponding assessment evidence
  at all — it is aspirational, not measured.
- **Orphan assessment.** A heavily weighted assessment item maps to no stated goal.
- **Activity without assessment signal.** A major learning activity produces no
  artefact that feeds any assessment, so learners deprioritise it.
- **Granularity mismatch.** A single holistic grade is asked to certify several
  distinct competencies that need separate evidence.

For each flagged pattern the skill names the specific corner conflict, gives the
mechanism by which it misleads learners, and proposes a lift — it does not silently
rewrite the lecturer's rubric.

## Interaction with process assessment

The "process goal, product assessment" pattern is the most common in CBE because
the framework is process-heavy. Its repair draws on the process-artefact catalogue
in `methodology-005-process-assessment-patterns.md`.
