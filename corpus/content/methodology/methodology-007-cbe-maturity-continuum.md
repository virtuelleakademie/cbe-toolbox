# The CBE Maturity Continuum: Three Levels Across the Seven PIONEER Criteria

> **Provenance.** Grounded in the validated PIONEER Working Package 4 source
> *4.2.1 Sub-Task Didactical Toolbox — Train-the-Trainer*, §4.4 (Table 1) and
> §4.5. Provenance is **PIONEER WP4**, not "the CBE website". The maturity model
> is a developmental continuum, not a fixed format or a grading scale.

## Summary

PIONEER WP4 conceptualises CBE as a **developmental continuum** rather than a fixed model. The continuum distinguishes **exactly three levels of maturity** — *emerging CBE*, *good-practice CBE*, and *fully developed CBE* — read against the **seven PIONEER criteria** (`foundations-005`), yielding a 3×7 raster. Institutions and programmes may gradually grow toward more mature forms of CBE depending on their educational context, institutional readiness, and ecosystem collaboration. The levels are an analytical framework for reflection, comparison, and development — not a prescription. §4.5 frames CBE quality in two tiers, minimum standards and excellence criteria, which grounds the minimum/maximum reading of the model.

## The three maturity levels and their synonyms

The maturity table distinguishes between three levels. Each level carries several names in the handbook; they are **synonyms of one level, not additional levels**. A reader can resolve any handbook label to one of the three:

| Level | Framework names (this level, multiple names) | Student-perspective framing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Level 1 — CBE light / challenge-inspired** | Emerging CBE · CBE Light | Challenge **inspired** learning |
| **Level 2 — CBE good practice / challenge-oriented** | CBE Good Practice · good-practice CBE | Challenge **oriented** learning |
| **Level 3 — full-fledged CBE / transformative** | CBE Full-Fledged · Strong CBE · fully developed CBE | Challenge **based** learning |

So: **Level 1 = CBE light = challenge-inspired = emerging**; **Level 2 = CBE good practice = challenge-oriented = good-practice**; **Level 3 = full-fledged CBE = transformative = fully-developed = Strong CBE.** Every handbook label resolves unambiguously to one of these three levels. There is no five-label scale — the five labels circulating in earlier drafts are synonym pairs across these three levels.

## The 3×7 raster of Table 1

For each of the seven PIONEER criteria, the table gives a per-cell maturity cue describing what the design looks like at each level.

| PIONEER criterion | Level 1 — Emerging / CBE Light | Level 2 — CBE Good Practice | Level 3 — Full-Fledged / Strong CBE |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Societal (real-world) challenge** | A real-world topic is referenced, but the challenge is predefined and closed. | Students work on an open-ended challenge rooted in a real societal or regional issue. | The challenge originates from a real stakeholder, is genuinely open-ended, and aims to create societal value beyond the course. |
| **Stakeholder involvement** | Stakeholders appear as guest speakers or case providers. | Stakeholders interact with students at defined moments (e.g. briefing, feedback). | Stakeholders act as challenge owners or co-creators throughout the learning process. |
| **Inter-/transdisciplinary** | Students are encouraged to consider other perspectives, but without structural integration. | Multiple disciplines or knowledge domains are explicitly integrated. | Academic and non-academic knowledge is co-created in a transdisciplinary manner and structurally embedded. |
| **Student agency & role of the teacher** | Students have limited choices within a predefined framework. | Students make key decisions; teachers act mainly as facilitators. | Students fully own the challenge process; teachers primarily enable, coach, and reflect. |
| **Iterative learning process** | Some practical activities, but limited iteration or feedback. | Structured learning phases with feedback and reflection loops. | Multiple iterative cycles of testing, feedback, reflection, and adaptation are central. |
| **Value-orientation, reflection** | Value-oriented reflection mentioned but not systematically addressed. | Students reflect on ethical implications and societal impact of their solutions. | Ethical reflection and value considerations are embedded throughout the challenge process. |
| **Societal impact and transferability** | Potential societal relevance is discussed but not acted upon. | Outputs are shared with stakeholders or relevant communities. | Outputs contribute to real change, decision-making, or follow-up actions beyond the course. |

*Table 1 — CBE maturity levels definition (PIONEER WP4, 4.2.1 §4.4).*

## Developmental-continuum framing

CBE is presented as a **developmental and flexible model rather than a fixed educational format**. The table presents three *illustrative* levels along a continuum; the levels were derived from the experiences of PIONEER partners and from examples collected across participating institutions. Their purpose is **not to prescribe a single model of implementation** but to provide an **analytical framework** for understanding different stages of CBE development, supporting reflection, comparison, and the further development of institutional CBE capacity.

Institutions and programmes may gradually grow toward more mature forms of CBE depending on their **educational context, institutional readiness, and ecosystem collaboration**. The three levels represent progressively greater degrees of openness, student autonomy, and complexity in learning design; partners (institutions, educators, and students) can gradually develop their capacity to work with CBE.

## Toolbox mandate

The document states its own applied purpose explicitly: *the maturity continuum, together with the stages developed within the PIONEER alliance (WP4, didactical toolbox subgroup), will inform the AI-based didactical toolbox intended to support lecturers in the design, redesign, and enhancement of their modules.* This maturity continuum is therefore the grounding the toolbox reads maturity against.

## Minimum standards and excellence criteria

To ensure consistency across institutions and partners, §4.5 structures CBE quality into **two levels: minimum standards and excellence criteria**. This is the source of the model's minimum/maximum vocabulary: the **minimum** is the baseline a design must reach to qualify as CBE; the **maximum** of the model is the excellence level (Level 3 / full-fledged CBE).

The **minimum standards** — the baseline for acceptable CBE practice — are:

- a clearly defined real-world challenge;
- student collaboration in teams;
- at least one form of formative feedback cycle;
- a basic reflection activity (individual or group-based);
- use of a structured assessment framework (e.g. rubric or portfolio elements);
- some level of interdisciplinary input.

§4.5 names the two quality tiers and enumerates the **minimum standards**; a fuller enumeration of the excellence criteria is **not** part of the validated document and is left out here. The excellence tier corresponds to the fully developed / Level 3 end of the continuum above.
