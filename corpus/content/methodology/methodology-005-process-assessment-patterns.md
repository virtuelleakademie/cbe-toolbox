# Process Assessment Patterns: Artefacts and Rubrics for Process-Oriented CBE

## Summary

When a CBE goal is process-oriented, assessment needs process artefacts and process-sensitive rubrics; otherwise the rubric rewards only the final product and the process goal goes unmeasured.

## Process-artefact catalogue

Artefacts that make process visible and therefore assessable:

- **Decision logs** — dated records of design or research decisions with the
  reasoning, capturing the germane work directly.
- **Iteration trails** — successive prototype/draft versions with change rationale,
  evidencing iterative competence.
- **Reflection entries** — structured reflection feeding the Reflect-Document-Share
  cross-cutting process (`methodology-001`).
- **Guiding-question maps** — the learner's research agenda and how it evolved.
- **Synthesis memos** — interim sense-making that shows analysis, not just a final
  conclusion.
- **Peer- and self-assessment records** — process evidence the learner co-produces.

## Rubric patterns

- **Weighting parity** — when the goal is process-heavy, the rubric weight on
  process artefacts must reflect that; a 70/30 product/process split for a
  process-dominant goal is a mis-alignment.
- **Criterion traceability** — every rubric criterion maps to a stated competence
  (guards against orphan-assessment and unassessed-goal patterns from
  `methodology-003`).
- **Formative checkpoints** — low-stakes process checkpoints (Black & Wiliam's
  formative-assessment tradition) before the summative artefact.
- **Separated evidence** — distinct competencies get distinct evidence rather than
  one holistic grade (guards against the granularity-mismatch pattern).
