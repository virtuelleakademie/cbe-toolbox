# Heterogeneity and Scaffolding: Composition and Role-Differentiation Patterns

## Summary

Cohort heterogeneity spans several axes (prior-knowledge spread, methodological readiness, self-regulation, disciplinary and cultural plurality); team composition and role differentiation are the two structural levers a CBE design uses to scaffold across that spread.

## The spread axes

Heterogeneity is not one dimension. The skill's question elicits at least:

- **Prior-knowledge spread** — how far apart the most and least prepared learners
  are on the targeted domain content.
- **Dimension axes** — *domain* knowledge, *methodological* readiness (research,
  tooling), and *self-regulation* capacity can each be spread independently.
- **Disciplinary / cultural plurality** — mixed programmes or international cohorts
  bring different baseline assumptions about what counts as evidence or rigour.

Scaffolding tuned to the wrong axis (e.g. domain support when the real spread is in
self-regulation) misses. The question surfaces the axis before proposing a lever.

## Composition options

- **Heterogeneous mixed teams** — deliberately spread prior knowledge across teams.
  Strength: peer explanation, ZPD-style support. Risk: free-riding, expert capture.
- **Homogeneous tiered teams** — group by readiness, differentiate the challenge
  scope per tier. Strength: targeted challenge level. Risk: signalling, reduced
  cross-pollination.
- **Hybrid with role rotation** — mixed teams plus rotating structural roles so the
  same learners do not always occupy the same function.

Each is a defensible alternative with a tradeoff, presented as options — never as
the assistant's preferred answer.

## Role-differentiation patterns

Within a team, structural roles distribute the cognitive and coordination load:
facilitator, researcher/synthesiser, documenter (feeds the Reflect-Document-Share
process), external-liaison (for the authentic Act-phase audience). Role rotation
across challenge phases prevents the strongest learner from monopolising the
germane work.
