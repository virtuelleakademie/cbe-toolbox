### Reflection Design

Students fill in a self-assessment form during the kick-off event of the module. They then are asked about every 2-3 weeks to reflect on their progress, learnings, challenges, etc. in a personal online journal. At the end of the module, they again fill in a self-assessment and reflect on the achievement of the goals they set themselves in the opening assessment. All these reflections then go into their concluding self-reflection which is graded.

rflect.ch is used by the students in this module. This tool sends invitations and reminders for each reflection task, and offers an optional AI tutor to go deeper in their journal entries.

## Phase-by-Phase Walkthrough

### Phase 1 – Engage

Before the module starts, students select their preference for the field of the challenge (structural engineering or rainwater management). The instructors then put students into groups.

There is a kick-off event at the beginning of the module. Students are introduced to their challenges by instructors from the respective fields. Furthermore, they receive some instructions and coaching on project management, teamwork, communication, pitching, etc.

### Phase 2 – Investigate

Students use methods they know from various modules and their work experience to analyse the project task and location, stakeholders, techniques, etc. Some do on-site investigations in Lauterbrunnen.

### Phase 3 – Act / Prototype

Students are free on how to organise themselves as a group to come up with suggestions, plans, options for a solution. They meet for several coaching sessions with the instructor of their field as well as an interim review.

### Phase 4 – Reflect & Present

After handing their plans, all groups meet to present their solution and to answer their fellow students’ and instructors’ questions. They then compile their reflections and write a personal concluding reflection.

## Instructor Perspective

### Preparation Required

Find a suitable project/location and design the challenge

Instruct the instructors of the two fields about the module framework, details, requirements, assessment criteria, etc.

Design a learning platform on an LMS such as Moodle

Organise students into groups based on certain criteria

Organise a kick-off event as well as coaching sessions, interim reviews, and a presentation event

### Instructor Role During Module

After handing out the challenges, instructors mainly are facilitators and coaches; they provide expertise based on the students’ suggestions and questions. During the interim reviews, they shift into the fictional role of the client where they are more critical and less supportive than in the coaching sessions. Finally, they take the role of assessors once the students have handed in their solutions.

### Challenges & Tips

It proved difficult to give students a task that is open enough to give them room for creativity and their own ideas on the one hand, and detailed enough to give them the confidence that they are doing what is required of them on the other. Students often asked for a more detailed task – instructors, however, invited them to come up with their own suggestions.

Group dynamics and the free-riding phenomenon are a challenge. Focusing on cooperation and group work as well as communication in the kick-off event helps, as does a good knowledge of students’ personalities when putting together the groups.

Students tend to be confused by the role shift of the instructors for the interim reviews. This shift should be clearly communicated to them in advance.

### Variations / Adaptations

Involving stakeholders from the real project and/or having students present their solutions to the municipality Lauterbrunnen would be a huge plus for students’ motivation – as well as the instructors’ workload.

Involving other disciplines such as architecture would also make the project mor realistic as well as complex. Interdisciplinary teamwork, however, is part of another module in the study programme.

## Outcomes & Transferability

### Student Experience

> Practice observation, not empirically grounded. The following account is retained from the supplied module template; no independent outcome evidence is added.

Students appreciated the module as a preparation for future project work both in their curriculum as well as their career: to deal with more complex tasks, to organise themselves as a group and as an individual (project management), to apply competences from various modules, to render their solution compatible with that of one group from the other field.

### Transferability

The general framework of the module (kick-off, coaching, group work, interim review, presentations, self-reflection) can easily be adapted in other programmes and for other kinds of projects of comparable size. As a high amount of coordination is necessary between the different instructors involved, it is easier to stage the module in a programme where the different instructors work closely together on a regular basis. Being familiar with using a LMS platform helps.

## Translated Challenge Appendix

The two challenge texts below are faithful English translations of the German appendix.

### Structural engineering challenge

As part of the planned redesign and improvement of the church square in Lauterbrunnen and the sustainable improvement of local parking, an underground two-storey parking facility is planned. At ground level above it, the car-park exit and a bus terminal with a forecourt for public transport are planned.

The site is strongly shaped by its topography and limited space. It is bounded by the Herrenbächli stream to the south and the steep, winding road toward Stechelberg to the north. The gradient rises from about 2% around the village and church square to about 10% along the road. Nearby reference points include the Staubbach waterfall about 100 metres above the site and the Lauterbrunnen church about 10 metres from the south-eastern edge.

The task focuses on developing an appropriate and innovative bus terminal and car-park exit with a roof. The load-bearing structure must respond to the underground-car-park structure, meet functional and construction requirements, support the new use of the square, fit sensitively into Lauterbrunnen’s built and landscape context, and improve the quality of the church square.

### Urban drainage and rainwater-management challenge

The same redesign includes a two-storey underground car park with a bus terminal for public transport at ground level. The task is to design the drainage for the planned bus terminal, considering contemporary rainwater-management approaches, the sponge-city concept and the effects of climate scenarios.

Students must work into topics that have only partly been covered in class, as often happens in practice: learn a new area, gain an overview, structure the commission, compare options and develop a proposed solution. They may also consider interfaces and dependencies with affected areas, including the structural-engineering part of Projektarbeit 1.
