# W.A.Ve. — Workshop Architettura Venezia

> **Collection note.** This project-authored case preserves the supplied module account, its distinctions and its visible gaps. The supplied office or PDF file and the assessment workbook are not bibliographic sources.

## Summary

An intensive, full-immersion summer architectural workshop model. International curators, mentors, and students gather for three weeks to rapidly generate high-concept spatial answers to urgent global architectural and societal themes.

## Module Identity

### Module Title

W.A.Ve. (Workshop Architettura Venezia) Model

### Short Description

An intensive, full-immersion summer architectural workshop model. International curators, mentors, and students gather for three weeks to rapidly generate high-concept spatial answers to urgent global architectural and societal themes.

### University / Institution

Università Iuav Venezia

### Program Level

BA

### Program / Discipline

Architecture

### ECTS Credits

4-6

### Duration

3 weeks of intensive block format (full-time, daily engagement, usually on July).

### Cohort Size

900 (800 local students; 100 international students)

## Delivery Architecture

### Delivery Form

On-site

### Delivery Format

Intensive Summer Workshop

### Institutional Constraints

Management of short-term international student enrollment and fast-tracked credit recognition pathways under tight academic deadlines; execution of large-scale international welcoming

### Design Constraints

Massive institutional spatial management (all campus spaces converted into studios simultaneously); intensive material procurement (wood, cardboard, foam); coordination of a large-scale final public exhibition event and international guest logistics.

## Challenge & Stakeholders

### Challenge Topic

High-impact, forward-looking architectural concepts responding to acute societal and environmental shifts under "post-sustainability" frameworks (e.g., W.A.Ve. 2025: "Labor. New Workplaces, Theories and Forms of Collaboration"; W.A.Ve. 2024: "Walkable Architectures").

### Challenge Formulation

"How can swift architectural imagination and radical design actions repurpose obsolete spatial typologies and construct new collaborative landscapes for contemporary civic or material communities?"

### Challenge Authenticity / Origin

Co-created with international university networks and global architecture masters to blend localized geographical problems with cutting-edge architectural theories.

### Challenge Owner / Stakeholders

Municipalities, international cultural bodies (e.g., Venice Biennale), research alliances, and regional socio-economic associations.

### Stakeholder Involvement Moments

final public exhibition

## Constructive Alignment

### Intended Learning Outcomes

Not supplied.

### Key Learning Activities

- Formulate a highly creative, conceptual architectural solution responding to an open-ended cultural or environmental challenge under extreme time constraints.

- Collaborate seamlessly in massive, fast-paced international student teams, managing cultural and linguistic differences.

- Execute rapid physical and visual prototyping using analog modeling and advanced spatial graphics.

- Pitch and defend complex design concepts concisely to an international jury of prominent architects and scholars.

### Assessment Criteria / Rubrics

Conceptual originality and radical architectural vision; clarity and professional impact of visual representation; high-fidelity quality of physical scale models; effectiveness of the team's final pitch.

### Assessment Evidence & Feedback

Professional graphic design panels, a large-scale physical studio model, and a synchronized group oral pitch. Feedback is swift, daily, and highly visual, delivered directly via studio-wide reviews.

### Reflection Design

Daily end-of-day collective studio debriefs, informal evening curator discussions

## Phase-by-Phase Walkthrough

### Phase 1 – Engage

Enthusiastic launch. The university transforms into a unified workshop environment. Students are assigned to international ateliers, meet their guest curators, absorb the challenge theme, and conduct immediate spatial surveys.

### Phase 2 – Investigate

Hyper-accelerated diagnostic phase. Rapid conceptual mapping, historical tracking of themes, typological brainstorming, and swift identification of spatial leverage points.

### Phase 3 – Act / Prototype

The core design sprint (Week 2 to Week 3). Intense studio work focused on continuous drawing, fast-paced prototyping, and massive scale physical modeling.

### Phase 4 – Reflect & Present

Final week. Massive transformation of the university building into a public exhibition gallery. Teams mount their boards, set up their scale models, and present their work during the public jury evaluation event.

## Instructor Perspective

### Preparation Required

Curation and booking of international guest professors months in advance; setting up customized registration portals for incoming international students; bulk ordering massive amounts of modeling materials; re-organizing university spaces.

### Instructor Role During Module

Shifts from a traditional academic to an active motivational coach who maintains high team morale and energy under strict deadlines.

### Challenges & Tips

Divide the atelier into specialized micro-task units (e.g., model unit, graphics unit, pitch unit) early on to ensure parallel efficiency and total student inclusion.

### Variations / Adaptations

Scalable into cross-disciplinary activities (e.g. BIPs). As a piloting experience one of the workshops of WAVE 2026 is expected to be framed as a BIP with Pioneer partners.

## Outcomes & Transferability

### Student Experience

> Practice observation, not empirically grounded. The following account is retained from the supplied module template; no independent outcome evidence is added.

Students describe W.A.Ve. as a memorable milestone that boosts professional agility, builds deep international networks, and proves their ability to produce top-tier work under extreme constraints.

### Transferability

The "Intensive Block" format can be exported to any higher education ecosystem seeking to foster rapid innovation, break down strict disciplinary siloes, and implement high-impact, short-term international student engagement.
