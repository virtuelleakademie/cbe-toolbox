# User Experience & Information Visualization

> **Collection note.** This project-authored case preserves the supplied module account, its distinctions and its visible gaps. The supplied office or PDF file and the assessment workbook are not bibliographic sources.

## Summary

This project-based module challenges students to design and prototype innovative digital solutions for real societal problems. Working in interdisciplinary teams, students apply user-centred design, data visualisation and interaction design principles to develop a functional web or mobile application prototype in response to a challenge brief provided by external stakeholders (e.g., Refood, Evita Cancer)

## Challenge & Stakeholders

### Challenge Topic

Digital Innovation for Social Impact

### Challenge Formulation

Each partner proposes a real unsolved problem. Refood: optimize volunteer coordination & food rescue logistics. Evita Cancro: platform to allow the identification of hereditary risk of cancer. Teams design a web/mobile prototype addressing the partner’s needs.

### Challenge Authenticity / Origin

Real challenges sourced directly from partner organisations (co-designed kick-off brief).

### Challenge Owner / Stakeholders

Refood, Evita Cancro, and similar social institutions. End users (volunteers, patients, families) are secondary stakeholders

### Stakeholder Involvement Moments

1) Kick-off briefing – partners present challenge. 2) Mid-point feedback – teams present user research & low-fi prototypes. 3) Final jury – partners attend final presentations and may provide users for testing.

## Constructive Alignment

### Intended Learning Outcomes

1. Investigate user needs and the implications of social context for interaction and experience.

2. Design interactive experiences across multiple channels (mobile, web, smart environments).

3. Apply data visualisation principles to create effective analytical interfaces.

4. Prototype innovative human-computer interactions adapted to different contexts.

5. Evaluate the usability of interfaces and the user experience.

### Key Learning Activities

- Engage: lectures on UX/UI, data vis, design thinking + analysis of partner briefs.

- Investigate: user research (questionnaires, interviews, observation) to validate needs.

- Act/Prototype: iterative lowfi → highfi interactive prototypes (Figma, Adobe XD) with data visualisation dashboards.

- Reflect/Present: team retrospectives, peer feedback, final project video + individual oral presentation.

### Assessment Criteria / Rubrics

- Group project (45%): final report, highfi prototype, video. Criteria: user research quality, prototype design, feasibility, responsiveness to stakeholder feedback.

- Individual oral presentation (45%): each student presents their specific contribution, answers questions, reflects on learning.

- Group data visualisation exercise (10%): practical exercise applying visualisation principles.

### Assessment Evidence & Feedback

- Evidence: report, prototype, video, individual oral performance.

- Feedback: formative feedback from instructors during lab sessions and at two mandatory checkpoints (weeks 7 & 11) + direct feedback from partners at midpoint.

### Reflection Design

- Midpoint feedback session (teams reflect on user research findings).

- Individual oral presentation includes a critical reflection on learning process and team contribution.

## Phase-by-Phase Walkthrough

### Phase 1 – Engage

Lectures on UX/UI + data visualisation. Kickoff where partners (Refood, Evita Cancro) present challenges. Students form teams (34), select a challenge, submit initial statement of work.

### Phase 2 – Investigate

Teams conduct user research (interviews, surveys, literature, usercentred methods) to define personas & user journeys. Submit a preliminary report on methodology and initial findings.

### Phase 3 – Act / Prototype

Iterative prototyping: lowfidelity wireframes → inclass peer/instructor feedback → highfidelity interactive prototype (web/mobile) with embedded information visualisation.

### Phase 4 – Reflect & Present

Teams finalise prototype, produce a 35 min project video, submit final report and prototype. Individual online oral presentations where each student presents, answers questions, and reflects. Partners may attend final presentations for closing feedback.

## Instructor Perspective

### Preparation Required

Before module start: 1) recruit & contract partner organisations (Refood, Evita Cancro, etc.). 2) Codefine actionable challenge brief with each partner. 3) Schedule all partner touchpoints (kickoff, midpoint, final jury). 4) Prepare lectures, activities, detailed student brief. 5) Set up submission portals and rubrics in the university’s elearning platform.

### Instructor Role During Module

Primary role: facilitator & coach. Shifts from content expert (early lectures) to project manager/mentor as teams work independently on their challenges.

### Challenges & Tips

- Challenge: securing committed partners available for feedback sessions. Tip: build a small network of reliable partners over time; keep internal “backup” challenges.

- Challenge: managing team dynamics and equal contribution. Tip: use peer evaluation forms + enforce mandatory 60% attendance rule.

### Variations / Adaptations

- Interdisciplinary: invite students from design or social sciences.

- COIL/BIP: collaborate with a partner university abroad → joint final presentation week.

- Scale: use fewer partners and allow multiple teams per challenge.

## Outcomes & Transferability

### Student Experience

> Practice observation, not empirically grounded. The following account is retained from the supplied module template; no independent outcome evidence is added.

Students value realworld impact and direct partner interaction. Most transformative aspect: designing for a tangible purpose beyond a grade. Increased motivation, deeper UX/UI learning, improved teamwork and communication. Midpoint feedback and final presentations are cited as intense but crucial learning moments.

### Transferability

Highly transferable to other disciplines (Product Design, Business, Digital Marketing, Data Science).

Required conditions:

- Willing partners with clear, nonconfidential, projectsized challenges.

- Institutional commitment to flexible projectbased assessment.

- Instructors comfortable with an openended, facilitative teaching role.
