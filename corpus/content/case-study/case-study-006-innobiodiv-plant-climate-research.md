# InnoBioDiv — Innovative Research on Plant–Environment Interaction

> **Collection note.** This project-authored case preserves the supplied module account, its distinctions and its visible gaps. The supplied office or PDF file and the assessment workbook are not bibliographic sources.

## Summary

This interdisciplinary module enables students from biology and engineering to collaboratively explore the impact of climate change on plant growth and soil biodiversity using robotics (farm.bot) and sensor technologies. Students develop and conduct experiments, analyze data, and present scientific results in a real-world under an international setting.

## Module Identity

### Module Title

Innovative Research on Plant Environment Interaction in a Changing Climate (InnoBioDiv)

### Short Description

This interdisciplinary module enables students from biology and engineering to collaboratively explore the impact of climate change on plant growth and soil biodiversity using robotics (farm.bot) and sensor technologies. Students develop and conduct experiments, analyze data, and present scientific results in a real-world under an international setting.

### University / Institution

University of Cologne (UzK) & TH Köln (THK)

### Program Level

Bachelor (Biology) / Master (Engineering) / PhD (Ukraine Cooperation Program)

### Program / Discipline

Biology / Engineering / Digital Communication Technology & IoT

### ECTS Credits

12 ECTS (Biology Bachelor) / 5 ECTS (Engineering Master)

### Duration

7 weeks, block course, total workload according to the ECTS shown above.

### Cohort Size

Approx. 6 students per interdisciplinary group (group size), multiple groups running in parallel; exact total depends on enrolment

## Delivery Architecture

### Delivery Form

Mostly on-site with hybrid elements for international cooperation, video conferences, lab work

### Delivery Format

Research-based learning in interdisciplinary groups; includes lectures, hands-on experiments, progress meetings, scientific reporting, and presentations

### Institutional Constraints

ECTS credit regulations vary by institution, academic calendars aligned. Language requirements due to international collaboration (English C1) Mobility parts supported online and by Erasmus+.

### Design Constraints

- lab space in a greenhouse or phyto chamber for the farmbot system

- stable server infrastructure (PostgreSQL, Grafana)

- group size limited to about 6 for effective collaboration

- budget for hardware maintenance, code is based on public domain software.

## Challenge & Stakeholders

### Challenge Topic

Effects of climate change on plant growth and soil biodiversity

### Challenge Formulation

How can IoT, robotics, and sensor technologies be integrated to be able to scientifically investigate and monitor the effects of climate change on plant and soil ecosystems in a controlled experimental setup?

### Challenge Authenticity / Origin

Real-world environmental research question co-designed by biology and engineering faculties as part of a joint research initiative (InnoBioDiv)

### Challenge Owner / Stakeholders

University departments (Biology, Digital Communications and IoT), scientific staff overseeing experiments, participating students, international partner universities

### Stakeholder Involvement Moments

Kick-off lectures, weekly progress meetings including faculty feedback, final scientific report presentation, and defence colloquium open to the academic public

## Constructive Alignment

### Intended Learning Outcomes

- Analyze the impact of climate change on biological systems using empirical data

- Design and conduct interdisciplinary scientific experiments integrating biology and sensor technology

- Apply Python programming and machine learning for data processing and analysis

- Collaborate effectively in interdisciplinary and international teams

- Communicate scientific findings in written reports and oral presentations

### Key Learning Activities

- Participating in introductory lectures on relevant biological and technical topics

- Designing and conducting soil and plant growth experiments using a farmbot gardening robot systems and sensors

- Programming and data analysis sessions (Python, machine learning, image processing)

- Weekly progress meetings and group discussions

- Preparing and delivering final scientific reports and presentations

### Assessment Criteria / Rubrics

- Scientific quality and rigor of experiments

- Quality of data collection and analysis

- Clarity and professionalism of reports and presentations

- Collaboration and teamwork effectiveness

- Ability to defend findings in colloquium

### Assessment Evidence & Feedback

Students submit a scientific report and present results weekly at as a final presentation. The faculty provides formative feedback during weekly meetings and final summative evaluation post-defence. Biology students must write a final exam in addition.

### Reflection Design

Reflection integrated via weekly group discussions, peer feedback, and final self-assessment within the presentation phase. The project is evaluated by feedback forms and open text. The teaching groups analyses the results and try to permanently improve the course offer.

## Phase-by-Phase Walkthrough

### Phase 1 – Engage

Introduction to the challenge via lectures on climate change, plant biology, sensor technology, and relevant programming skills. Formation of interdisciplinary groups

### Phase 2 – Investigate

Students research relevant literature, develop experimental designs, learn to operate the farmbot and the sensors, and enroll in Python and ML courses.

### Phase 3 – Act / Prototype

Implementation of experiments with real-time data collection and iteration of experimental parameters. Routine Friday progress meetings for troubleshooting and feedback

### Phase 4 – Reflect & Present

Analysis and visualization of collected data, preparation of scientific reports, final presentations and defense colloquium with other groups, supervisors, and faculty audience

## Instructor Perspective

### Preparation Required

Scheduling partner institutions and ensuring technical infrastructure readiness (farmbot, sensors, servers). Preparation of introductory lectures and programming instruction materials. Coordination of the international cooperation. Advertising within the participating faculties.

### Instructor Role During Module

Facilitator and coach guiding interdisciplinary collaboration, technical advisor supporting hardware and software use, scientific advisor evaluating experimental rigor, coordinator of timelines and meetings

### Challenges & Tips

Support of the group building and the communication inside the group.

Balancing the interdisciplinary knowledge gap between biology and engineering students.

Coordination of the cross-university scheduling,

Maintaining engagement in the intensive block format.

Ensuring data quality and experimental reproducibility

### Variations / Adaptations

Module scalable to different cohort sizes. In principle, adaptable to fully online or hybrid delivery if a remote control of the experiments can be implemented and there is staff available on-site for cultivation, care, harvesting, and analysis of the plants ( However, this form is less desirable)

Content customizable to other environmental or technical challenges; integration of additional disciplines possible

## Outcomes & Transferability

### Student Experience

> Practice observation, not empirically grounded. The following account is retained from the supplied module template; no independent outcome evidence is added.

Students report valuable interdisciplinary collaboration and hands-on learning. The challenges include mastering diverse skill sets and effective teamwork providing transformative insights gained at the intersection of biology and technology (see publication under https://cos.bibl.th-koeln.de/frontdoor/deliver/index/docId/1314/file/Prototyp_Zukunft_FIHB_Band_23.pdf)

### Transferability

The module’s structure and interdisciplinary approach can be adopted by other institutions focusing on environmental science, engineering, or data science; prerequisites include access to relevant technical infrastructure and collaborative partnerships.
