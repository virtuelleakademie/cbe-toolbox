# Integrated Design Laboratories / Laboratori Integrati

> **Collection note.** This project-authored case preserves the supplied module account, its distinctions and its visible gaps. The supplied office or PDF file and the assessment workbook are not bibliographic sources.

## Summary

A semester-long, multi-disciplinary studio model that integrates two or three distinct projecting disciplines. Students work in small collaborative teams inside dedicated classrooms for full days to address real-world, open-ended spatial and architectural challenges.

## Module Identity

### Module Title

Integrated Design Laboratories (Laboratori Integrati)

### Short Description

A semester-long, multi-disciplinary studio model that integrates two or three distinct projecting disciplines. Students work in small collaborative teams inside dedicated classrooms for full days to address real-world, open-ended spatial and architectural challenges.

### University / Institution

Università Iuav Venezia

### Program Level

BA/MA

### Program / Discipline

Architecture, Urban Planning, Design

### ECTS Credits

12-14

### Duration

1 semester (15–16 weeks) with a full-day weekly rhythm (students project together in the classroom for the entire day under faculty supervision).

### Cohort Size

Large institutional cohort subdivided into parallel thematic laboratories of approximately 50–80 students each, further organized into small teamwork groups.

## Delivery Architecture

### Delivery Form

On-site

### Delivery Format

Semester Module

### Institutional Constraints

Complex pedagogical and administrative coordination required to merge diverse student cohorts, specifically balancing the integration of local students, international exchange students (e.g., overcoming structural and linguistic barriers to ensure equitable teamwork within mixed groups; and managing the administrative alignment of varying prerequisite skills)

### Design Constraints

High demand for large physical workspace tables; mandatory long-term availability of digital fabrication and printing labs; strict requirements for regular full-day attendance from both students and teaching assistants.

## Challenge & Stakeholders

### Challenge Topic

Multidisciplinary urban regeneration, historic preservation, and sustainable structural retrofitting of real-world European or local territories facing socio-environmental fragilities.

### Challenge Formulation

The challenge formulation changes semester by semester.

### Challenge Authenticity / Origin

Challenges derive from localized territorial complexities, physical site conditions, and real-world urban constraints in collaboration with national and regional architectural heritage networks.

### Challenge Owner / Stakeholders

Local public administrations, territorial development agencies, historical heritage boards, and local civic committees.

### Stakeholder Involvement Moments

Initial context briefings and delivery of site data; physical site tours and community diagnostic interviews

## Constructive Alignment

### Intended Learning Outcomes

1) Analyze complex physical, historical, and structural contexts using multi-scalar analytical mapping and structural diagnostics.

2) Synthesize distinct disciplinary requirements (composition, structures, environmental technology) into a single, comprehensive architectural project.

3) Collaborate within stable, multi-member design teams to manage split responsibilities and complex project delivery.

4) Formulate and communicate detailed professional-grade execution portfolios, technical drawings, and architectural models.

### Key Learning Activities

Full-day active design studios, cross-disciplinary lectures, site surveying and technical diagnostic exercises, iterative scale physical modeling

### Assessment Criteria / Rubrics

Multi-disciplinary technical coherence; structural and technological feasibility; formal and spatial composition quality; team project portfolio completeness and clarity.

### Assessment Evidence & Feedback

Final comprehensive design portfolio (drawings, details, calculations) and a detailed scale model. Continuous formative feedback is provided via weekly iterative team-level critiques.

### Reflection Design

Mid-semester cross-team peer reviews, structured group self-assessment and final studio-wide debriefing panels.

## Phase-by-Phase Walkthrough

### Phase 1 – Engage

Generally, students explore the physical site, receive detailed technical briefs, and are introduced to conflicting disciplinary goals (e.g., aesthetics vs. structural limits) through site visits and stakeholder talks.

### Phase 2 – Investigate

Intensive data gathering, historical research, regulatory checkups, structural mapping, and technical diagnostics of the pre-existing site or urban framework.

### Phase 3 – Act / Prototype

Long iterative design phase in the studio. Teams draft technical drawings, test structural layouts, process digital and analog options, and build physical models to integrate multiple systems.

### Phase 4 – Reflect & Present

Final production of the professional execution portfolio and large-scale architectural models, presented and cross-examined in a classroom “defense”.

## Instructor Perspective

### Preparation Required

Pre-arranging spatial data, cartography, and structural baseline reports; aligning the syllabi and grading weights of the participating multidisciplinary co-teachers; reserving physical studio environments.

### Instructor Role During Module

Shifting continuously between a traditional content expert (delivering targeted technical lectures) and a design facilitator/studio coach (guiding structural compromises and team problem-solving).

### Challenges & Tips

Challenge: Frictions between different student backgrounds or conflicting goals of different disciplines.

Tip: Set early micro-deadlines requiring simultaneous structural and spatial sketches to force early multidisciplinary integration.

### Variations / Adaptations

Highly adaptable to interdisciplinary architecture modules, or urban planning masterclasses.

## Outcomes & Transferability

### Student Experience

> Practice observation, not empirically grounded. The following account is retained from the supplied module template; no independent outcome evidence is added.

Valued as a realistic simulation of a professional architecture office. Students find the heavy workload challenging but highly transformative for developing core technical synthesis and coordination skills.

### Transferability

Replicable in any higher education curriculum that demands the convergence of multiple scientific fields into a physical or material prototype (e.g., civil engineering, industrial design, environmental management).
