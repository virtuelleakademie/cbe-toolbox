# Model United Nations / UHUMUN

> **Collection note.** This project-authored case preserves the supplied module account, its distinctions and its visible gaps. The supplied office or PDF file and the assessment workbook are not bibliographic sources.

## Summary

A Model United Nations is a simulation of the United Nations system open to students from a range of disciplines and academic levels, in which participants act as delegates from different countries, taking part in debates and negotiations on issues that fall within the remit of the various United Nations bodies and committees.

## Module Identity

### Module Title

Model United Nations at the University of Huelva (UHUMUN)

### Short Description

A Model United Nations is a simulation of the United Nations system open to students from a range of disciplines and academic levels, in which participants act as delegates from different countries, taking part in debates and negotiations on issues that fall within the remit of the various United Nations bodies and committees.

### University / Institution

University of Huelva

### Program Level

BA / MA / PhD

It could be any of them, as it is open to the whole university community.

### Program / Discipline

An educational innovation project open to students from across the University

### ECTS Credits

Students are awarded 2.5 credits, which can be used to count towards an elective module on their degree course.

### Duration

To participate, students undertake an 8-hour preparatory course, comprising 4 hours of face-to-face sessions and 4 hours of online learning.

This is followed by a two-and-a-half-day programme, totalling 25 hours, divided into 8 working sessions and breaks for relaxation and rest in a large group setting.

### Cohort Size

Between 90 and 120 students. They may participate individually or in pairs. To do so, they may choose any of the three or four committees that are established each year: two or three with representatives from up to 30 countries, and a fourth, the Security Committee, with 15 representatives. The latter is the only committee that accepts individual participation only.

## Delivery Architecture

### Delivery Form

The preparation for the Model UN takes a total of 8 hours. Four of these are delivered in person, and the remaining four involve individual work using the materials provided via the university’s online platform.

The Model United Nations takes place in person over two and a half days (25 hours).

### Delivery Format

Live case**/ Simulation (we are not very sure)

### Institutional Constraints

The Model United Nations takes place annually during the second or third week of March. Registration opens in the second week of January, and in-person preparation sessions run from the second week of February until the week before the Model United Nations takes place.

In the four previous editions, the language used in all committees was exclusively Spanish; however, from 2026 onwards, we will have an English-language committee, which will focus on addressing challenges related to SDG 11.

Students do not receive a specific mark for their participation, but anyone who registers and participates actively during the various Model sessions will receive a certificate for 25 hours.

University students at any level of study, from our university or from other universities within or outside the European Union, are welcome to attend.

### Design Constraints

As previously mentioned, the maximum number of participants per committee is 60 (30 countries represented either individually or in pairs), except in the case of the Security Committee, where participation is on an individual basis and is limited to a maximum of 15 people.

The Model is open to students from the University of Huelva and any other university who are interested in experiencing this great learning opportunity.

We have various institutional supports in place for its implementation; however, for the time being, we do not have a specific budget to facilitate the travel of students living outside Huelva

## Challenge & Stakeholders

### Challenge Topic

The challenges currently facing humanity are addressed, all of which are linked to the SDGs and based on the topics discussed within the various United Nations committees, for example: governance and the elimination of gender-based violence; transforming our urban environments into inclusive and resilient cities; eliminating violence and bullying in educational settings; multilateralism and the maintenance of world peace, amongst many others.

### Challenge Formulation

You will take part as a delegate for your chosen country within the United Nations committee of your choice. In this committee, you will address a topic relating to an international challenge, which must be analysed, discussed and negotiated with the other countries represented, with the aim of jointly drafting a resolution similar to those produced at the United Nations.

To take part, you will need to research the country you are representing in depth, both in the present day and throughout its history. You will also need to have a thorough understanding of its multilateral relations, the role it plays on the global stage, and the position it would adopt on the topic in question. Furthermore, you will need to thoroughly research the topic to be discussed, how it is approached by the other countries represented in your committee, their strengths and weaknesses, as well as the potential alliances that may emerge from the negotiation and debate processes.

### Challenge Authenticity / Origin

The analysis focuses on trending issues in national and international politics that are currently being addressed by the United Nations. Annual topics are selected on this basis, taking into account the need to ensure that students from different levels and types of education can participate.

### Challenge Owner / Stakeholders

These are issues that affect everyone in different ways, but on a widespread scale.

### Stakeholder Involvement Moments

The student and teaching staff involved in the organisation, who already have extensive experience with the Model and the selected topics, are present at every stage of the Model to present, describe, guide and encourage the search for solutions, as well as to provide feedback on the final solution presented to each committee.

## Constructive Alignment

### Intended Learning Outcomes

To encourage UHU students and participating lecturers to:

- Commit to tackling the major challenges facing humanity in order to achieve sustainable development that benefits all the inhabitants of our planet, based on the work carried out in relation to the Sustainable Development Goals set out in the UN 2030 Agenda.

- Develop fundamental skills for their personal and professional growth, notably: critical and creative thinking, an intercultural and diversity-focused approach, critical and in-depth research, rhetoric, negotiation, peaceful conflict resolution, cooperation, consensus building and teamwork.

- Delve deeper into the analysis of issues of personal, state, national, regional and global interest, through an interdisciplinary approach guided by subject matter experts who will offer diverse perspectives on aspects often viewed through a narrow lens, yet which require and urgently demand a complex approach, recognising the need for dialogue and interdisciplinary work to address the very complexity of our contemporary societies.

- Commit to expanding their personal and professional skills, notably multilingualism, teamwork and peaceful conflict resolution, as key elements in meeting the demands of their profession.

### Key Learning Activities

Described in the Phase-by-Phase Walkthrough.

### Assessment Criteria / Rubrics

Active, focused and proactive participation, consistent with their country’s position, both individually and collectively, at the various stages of the debate.

Drafting of the country’s official position, demonstrating high standards of vocabulary, grammar and content, and reflecting thorough research into and understanding of the situation in the country represented.

Adherence to the protocol set out by the Model in each of its phases and stages.

### Assessment Evidence & Feedback

Students play an active role in the preparatory sessions, putting the procedures and rules of the Model United Nations into practice. This is observed by teachers and more experienced peers, who provide direct, ongoing and immediate feedback to the students, enabling them to improve their performance.

There are also sessions to provide support with the research portfolio that each country is required to submit. Based on an analysis of the materials gathered, the reference sources and the individual drafting of their country’s official position, potential improvements are outlined, with guidance and support provided throughout each participant’s personal process.

Throughout the Model sessions, students receive guidance and feedback from the chairs and secretaries of each committee, as well as from the main support figures: master faculties (senior tutors) and faculties (student mentors).

### Reflection Design

Students complete a final survey in which they answer a series of questions regarding their satisfaction and what they have learnt. The survey assesses both the preparation for and the delivery of the Model United Nations.

## Phase-by-Phase Walkthrough

### Phase 1 – Engage

Students attend an introductory session lasting around 30 minutes, during which the annual Model United Nations event is presented, along with the committees that will form part of it and the topics to be addressed in each committee. Interested students register and receive a confirmation email containing instructions on how to access the material on our University’s digital platform, as well as details regarding their participation in the face-to-face training sessions.

During this phase, students read the general information about the Model and begin preparing to act as delegates.

In-person participation takes place over two sessions, each lasting two hours. During these sessions, they receive additional information, clarify any queries that may arise from reading the materials, and take part in MUN simulations to experience all the processes involved and learn how to apply the protocol.

### Phase 2 – Investigate

As part of the materials provided via the institutional platform, students have access to a document that describes in detail the topic to be addressed in each of the committees. This document explains how the committee in question operates, outlines the topic to be discussed, provides guiding questions, sets out main topics and subtopics, as well as sources of information and actual resolutions previously adopted by the United Nations in relation to the topic in question.

Based on this, students prepare their research portfolio and their country’s official position, which must be read out during the first working session. During these sessions, students must listen carefully to the other countries, participate in debates, and form alliances to make progress on drafting the resolution—that is, the closing document of the Model UN in which the represented countries make commitments to address the challenge set out in the initial topic.

### Phase 3 – Act / Prototype

Over the course of eight working sessions, students progress from listening and exchanging ideas to forging alliances with other countries and jointly drafting a resolution.

Once a bloc’s resolution is ready, it must be submitted to the MUN General Secretariat, which, based on criteria regarding format and content in line with the committee’s guidelines, decides whether the resolution may be presented to the rest of the committee.

Resolutions that are initially approved are presented to the committee, put to a vote, and if they receive the support of half the countries in the committee plus one, work begins on the amendments, that is, changes to the resolution to ensure it is acceptable to all countries.

### Phase 4 – Reflect & Present

Once the amendments have been made, the resolution is read out for the final time, a new vote is taken country by country to ensure there is sufficient agreement, and the sessions are closed.

At the awards ceremony, students complete a satisfaction survey and record their most significant experiences, as well as the skills they believe they have developed through their participation in the Model.

Also, those who wish to do so have the opportunity to take the microphone and say whatever they like about their experience.

## Instructor Perspective

### Preparation Required

Event planning, including detailed venues, dates and timetables

Spaces for each of the Model’s committees over the two-and-a-half-day event: these require a computer, screen, sound system and the necessary furniture, particularly tables for the chair and secretary of each committee, as well as a lectern for individual student participation.

A platform containing all the necessary documentation, complete and accurate

Registration forms and country assignments

Satisfaction surveys and QR codes

Presentations and promotional materials

Welcome pack for each participant: name badge, lanyard, notepad, pen and folder

Name badges for the organising team and for each committee

Invitations to authorities and key figures in the relevant fields

### Instructor Role During Module

Both the teaching staff and the student organisers are responsible for monitoring the students’ progress, providing guidance when difficulties arise, answering questions and encouraging their participation.

### Challenges & Tips

The main challenge is to engage students in an experience that places high demands on skills they have not yet fully developed, such as conducting research, thinking critically about issues of global significance, working in an interdisciplinary and intercultural manner, public speaking, debating and negotiating. It is also difficult due to the length of the sessions, as students have to miss several of their other modules, which in some cases has a negative impact on their marks in those modules.

Over the years, as the rest of the university teaching staff have become familiar with the Model and realised how enriching this experience is for students, participation has gradually increased and become easier to facilitate.

Recommendations from students to their peers have also been key to encouraging attendance.

### Variations / Adaptations

As mentioned above, Model United Nations programmes are common practice in educational institutions around the world. Further information on this topic, as well as examples and useful materials for adapting the programme to different contexts, can be found on the United Nations website: https://www.un.org/en/mun

### Transferability

Model United Nations programmes are simulations that have been held in various countries around the world for decades, particularly at university level, but also in sixth form and secondary schools. For this reason, we believe they can be implemented at the universities that form part of the alliance. Similarly, we believe that students from these universities can participate in our Model, as an ideal opportunity to experience interculturality and interdisciplinarity in all their complexity.

We consider the following to be key requirements:

- Human resources:

o     Organising committee comprising staff and students from various academic disciplines, who are responsible for designing and promoting the new edition of the Model each year.

o    Participating students.

- Handbook for participation in UHUMUN

- Social media and apps for registration and promotion

- University platform with detailed information on the Model and each annual committee

- Classrooms for face-to-face training sessions

- Material resources:

o    Recognition for participants

o    Special prizes

o    Participation kit

o    Identification badges

- Food and drink

Suitable rooms for each of the committees with the necessary furniture and equipment
