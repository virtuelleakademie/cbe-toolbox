# SGM1 — Going Global

> **Collection note.** This project-authored case preserves the supplied module account, its distinctions and its visible gaps. The supplied office or PDF file and the assessment workbook are not bibliographic sources.

## Summary

This module engages students in a challenge-based, live industry case focused on international expansion strategy. Working in multicultural teams across BFH and HM, students collaborate virtually and through two study trips to develop actionable global market entry strategies for a real company.

## Module Identity

### Module Title

SGM1 – Going Global: International Strategy & Operations

### Short Description

This module engages students in a challenge-based, live industry case focused on international expansion strategy. Working in multicultural teams across BFH and HM, students collaborate virtually and through two study trips to develop actionable global market entry strategies for a real company.

### University / Institution

Bern University of Applied Sciences Business School (BFH) & Hochschule München Business School (HM)

### Program Level

BA- bachelor Advanced level specialisation/elective

### Program / Discipline

Business Administration / International Business

### ECTS Credits

6 ECTS

### Duration

14 weeks

Weeks 1 – 4 asynchronous refresher self study

Weeks 5-14 synchronous online sessions 08h15 – 11h40 weekly except during study trips (week 7& 14)

### Cohort Size

Approx. 60 – 80 students (mixed BFH & HM teams)

Similar cohort size from each institution

## Delivery Architecture

### Delivery Form

Hybrid (Virtual Exchange + Physical Mobility)

### Delivery Format

COIL + BIP + Live Case integrated in semester module (BFH)

### Institutional Constraints

Mandatory participation in study trips for course completion ( BFH), study regulations in Germany stipulate that participation may not be made mandatory

Group-based assessment (written report + defense presentation). At BFH we can allocate group grades since a key learning outcome is intercultural collaboration, at HM students must be offered the possibility of individual grading.

Academic calendars across two institutions are not aligned BFH begins in calendar week 8 and HM begins in calendar week 12

### Design Constraints

Dependence on live case partner availability

Budget/logistics for two study trips (HM ↔ BFH)

Managing multicultural team dynamics

Size capped at 40 students per institution

## Challenge & Stakeholders

### Challenge Topic

International market expansion strategy and global operations in the respective sector

### Challenge Formulation

How can the company successfully expand into a high-potential international market and implement a viable go-to-market strategy? This depends entirely on the live case company

### Challenge Authenticity / Origin

Real-world live case co-designed with an industry partner, including direct interaction and data input

### Challenge Owner / Stakeholders

Primary: industry partner and their stakeholders (each case is different)

Secondary: academic stakeholders

### Stakeholder Involvement Moments

Kick-off discussion as to industry partner’s needs, case formulation and case finalisation.

With students:

Kick-off briefing (live case introduction)

Q&A session with company

Final presentations (jury incl. company representatives)

## Constructive Alignment

### Intended Learning Outcomes

Analyse international business environments and global market opportunities

Design international expansion and entry strategies

Develop solutions for real-world international business challenges

Collaborate effectively in multicultural and virtual teams

Apply intercultural competence in professional contexts

### Key Learning Activities

Live case analysis (PESTLE, competitor, market selection)

Multicultural teamwork (virtual + physical)

Coaching sessions with instructors

Stakeholder interaction (industry partner)

Study trips and on-site collaboration

### Assessment Criteria / Rubrics

Analytical depth (market, strategy, environment)

Feasibility and practicality of recommendations

Quality of argumentation and structure

Team collaboration and integration of perspectives

Presentation and defense quality

### Assessment Evidence & Feedback

Written group report (50%)

Final presentation + defense (50%)

Continuous feedback via coaching sessions and milestone presentations

### Reflection Design

Team reflections during coaching sessions

Informal reflection during study trips

Learning through intercultural interaction and feedback loops

## Phase-by-Phase Walkthrough

### Phase 1 – Engage

Online kick-off session with team building and live case introduction

Initial interaction with industry partner

Formation of multicultural teams

### Phase 2 – Investigate

Market and environment analysis (PESTLE, competitor analysis)

Research on target markets and stakeholders

Jigsaw learning and knowledge exchange sessions

Q&A session with company

### Phase 3 – Act / Prototype

Development of global strategy and go-to-market plan

Iterative refinement through coaching sessions

Status presentations and peer feedback

### Phase 4 – Reflect & Present

Final presentations during study trip to HM Munich

Defense with company and lecturers

Reflection through experience and peer feedback

## Instructor Perspective

Coordinate schedules across institutions

Plan study trips (logistics, venues, activities)

Prepare teaching materials and digital platforms

### Instructor Role During Module

Facilitator of learning process

Coach for teams

Connector between students and industry partner

Evaluator of outputs

### Challenges & Tips

Managing multicultural team dynamics requires active facilitation

Ensure clear communication and expectations early

Align academic calendars carefully

Prepare contingency plans for hybrid delivery

### Variations / Adaptations

Can be adapted to fully virtual COIL (no travel)

Can scale to different disciplines (e.g., engineering, design)

Can use different industry partners or sectors

**German rationale (verbatim):**

Reale Live-Case-Firma als Challenge-Owner, direkter Kontakt, Jury mit Firmenvertretern, iteratives Coaching. Etwas schwächer bei systematischer Reflexion und echter Transdisziplinarität (v.a. Business).

**Faithful English translation:**

A real live-case company acts as challenge owner, with direct contact, a jury including company representatives, and iterative coaching. The design is somewhat weaker in systematic reflection and genuine transdisciplinarity, as it is primarily business-focused.
